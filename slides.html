Section 1: Problem Formulation

Historical Context & The Paradigm Shift

Geometric vs Holistic Modeling

❌ Traditional 1980s Geometric Approach

Explicit Landmark Extraction & Distance Ratios:

  • Methodology: Located explicit anatomical features (eye corners, nasal bridge width, chin curves) and computed geometric distance ratios.
  • Cascading Failure: If landmark detection failed due to slight head pose or shadow, the entire recognition pipeline collapsed.
  • Rigid Assumptions: Failed to generalize across natural expressions (smiles, frowns) which distort landmark geometry.
  • High Computation: Slow edge-detection heuristics and heuristic graph matching.

⚡ Turk & Pentland (1991) Paradigm Shift

Holistic Information-Theoretic Subspace:

  • Faces as 2D Patterns: Treats an entire face image as an intensity vector $\Gamma \in \mathbb{R}^D$ without isolated feature detectors.
  • Optimal Variance Capture: Uses Principal Component Analysis (PCA) to extract the principal directions that best capture variation across the face ensemble.
  • Subspace Compression: Faces occupy a tiny manifold inside the vast $D=4096$ pixel space. A compact basis of $K \approx 30\text{--}50$ eigenfaces retains $>88\%$ of information.
  • Real-time Speed: Low-dimensional vector projections take $< 2.5$ ms.

STAGE 3

Eigenfaces Basis

  • Reconstruct: $\hat{\Gamma} = \Psi + U \Omega$
  • Compute DFFS ($\epsilon_d$) & DIFS ($\epsilon_c$)
  • Apply dual thresholds $(\theta_f, \theta_c)$

Section 3: Mathematical Formulation

Subspace Vectorization & Mean Centering

Data Matrix Formulation

Vectorization & Mean Face Formulation

  • Image Vectorization: An image $I(x, y)$ of dimension $N \times N = 64 \times 64$ is unrolled row-by-row into: $$\Gamma_i \in \mathbb{R}^D, \quad \text{where } D = 64 \times 64 = 4096$$
  • Average Face Vector ($\Psi$): $$\Psi = \frac{1}{M} \sum_{i=1}^M \Gamma_i$$ Represents the global average anatomical template common to all human faces.
  • Mean-Centered Difference Vectors ($\Phi_i$): $$\Phi_i = \Gamma_i - \Psi$$ Removes the shared average illumination and geometry, isolating individual facial variations.
  • Data Matrix ($A$): $$A = [\Phi_1, \Phi_2, \dots, \Phi_M] \in \mathbb{R}^{D \times M} \quad (4096 \times 245)$$

Why Mean Centering is Computationally Mandatory

  • Origin Translation: PCA requires calculating covariance around the centroid. Subtracting $\Psi$ shifts the cloud of face vectors to the origin $\mathbf{0}$.
  • Prevents Degenerate 1st Component: If uncentered faces are used, the first principal eigenvector merely aligns with the average brightness of the dataset, wasting a critical dimension.
  • Linear Combination of Deviations: Every face can be expressed as the shared template plus a linear combination of differences: $$\Gamma \approx \Psi + \sum_{k=1}^K \omega_k u_k$$
  • Sample Covariance Matrix: $$C = \frac{1}{M} \sum_{i=1}^M \Phi_i \Phi_i^T = \frac{1}{M} A A^T \in \mathbb{R}^{D \times D}$$
Section 4: Core Innovation

The Turk-Pentland Covariance Reduction Trick

The $L = A^T A$ Dimensionality Reduction Proof

The Computational Bottleneck: $C = \frac{1}{M} A A^T$

Dimension: $D \times D = 4096 \times 4096 = 16,777,216$ elements.

Computational Cost: Eigendecomposition requires $\mathcal{O}(D^3) \approx 6.87 \times 10^{10}$ operations.

⚠️ Rank Deficient: With only $M=245$ images, maximum rank is $M-1=244$. At least $3851$ eigenvalues are identically zero!

The Surrogate Formulation: $L = A^T A$

Dimension: $M \times M = 245 \times 245 = 60,025$ elements.

Computational Cost: Eigendecomposition requires $\mathcal{O}(M^3) \approx 1.47 \times 10^{7}$ operations.

✓ 279.5× smaller matrix • >4,600× reduction in operations!

Step-by-Step Mathematical Proof of Equivalence

1. Define the Surrogate Eigensystem:

$$A^T A v_k = \mu_k v_k$$

2. Multiply both sides from the left by matrix $A$:

$$A (A^T A v_k) = A (\mu_k v_k)$$

3. Regroup using matrix associativity:

$$(A A^T)(A v_k) = \mu_k (A v_k)$$

4. Recognize the Full Eigenvalue Relation:

Setting $u_k = A v_k$, we obtain: $C u_k = \mu_k u_k$.

5. Normalize to Unit Orthonormal Basis:

$$u_k = \frac{A v_k}{\|A v_k\|_2}$$

Q.E.D.: $A v_k$ is an exact eigenvector of $A A^T$ associated with eigenvalue $\mu_k$.

Section 7: Decision Framework

The 4-Way Decision Rule (Section 3.2)

Dual Threshold Metric Formulation

Dual Distance Metrics & 4 Quadrants

  • Distance From Face Space (DFFS): $$\epsilon_d = \|\Gamma - \hat{\Gamma}\|_2 = \|(I - U U^T)(\Gamma - \Psi)\|_2$$ Measures orthogonal reconstruction residual. If $\epsilon_d \ge \theta_f$, the probe is not a human face.
  • Distance In Face Space (DIFS): $$\epsilon_c = \min_k \|\Omega - \Omega_k\|_2$$ Measures Euclidean distance to closest enrolled class centroid.
  • Quadrant 1 (Known Face): $\epsilon_d < \theta_f \land \epsilon_c < \theta_c \implies$ Recognized Subject
  • Quadrant 2 (Unknown Face): $\epsilon_d < \theta_f \land \epsilon_c \ge \theta_c \implies$ New Unenrolled Person
  • Quadrant 3 (Not a Face): $\epsilon_d \ge \theta_f \implies$ Rejected Non-Face Pattern

Section 9: Failure Mode Analysis

Robustness & Stress Testing Under Perturbations

Illumination, Noise, Blur & Occlusion

Critical Vulnerabilities & Limitations

  • Illumination Shifts (Primary Vulnerability): PCA captures total variance. Extreme directional side-lighting produces larger pixel variances than identity differences, skewing projection weights.
  • Pose Variations: Out-of-plane rotation ($>15^\circ$) misaligns eyes and mouth with the basis vectors, causing sharp rises in DIFS.
  • Additive Noise Resistance: Moderate Gaussian noise ($\sigma < 0.15$) is gracefully discarded because high-frequency white noise projects weakly onto top eigenfaces.
  • Occlusions: Sunglasses or masks inflate the reconstruction residual $\epsilon_d$, triggering false rejection as "Not a Face".

Section 11: Viva Examination

Viva Defense: Key Evaluator Questions & Answers

Anticipated Technical Inquiries

Q1: Why do eigenvectors of $A^T A$ yield eigenvectors of $A A^T$?

By multiplying $A^T A v_k = \mu_k v_k$ on the left by $A$, associativity yields $(A A^T)(A v_k) = \mu_k (A v_k)$. Hence $u_k = \frac{A v_k}{\|A v_k\|}$ is an exact eigenvector of the full covariance matrix with the identical eigenvalue.

Q2: Why do eigenfaces look "ghostly"?

Each eigenface is a linear combination of all centered training faces: $u_k = \sum v_{ki} \Phi_i$. Adding and subtracting face templates constructs high/low intensity variation maps highlighting lighting gradients, eye sockets, and facial contours.

Q3: How are $\theta_f$ and $\theta_c$ determined?

$\theta_f$ is set with a safety margin above the maximum training face reconstruction error ($\epsilon_d$) to accept face variance while rejecting non-faces. $\theta_c$ is determined from the intra-class dispersion to delineate enrolled from unseen individuals.

Q4: What was the main improvement introduced by Fisherfaces?

PCA is unsupervised and confuses lighting variance with identity. Belhumeur et al. (1997) used Linear Discriminant Analysis (LDA) to maximize between-class scatter over within-class scatter, rendering recognition immune to lighting shifts.
