What are "Eigenfaces"?

Eigenfaces are the principal eigenvectors of the face covariance matrix $C = \frac{1}{M} A A^T$. Each eigenface represents an orthogonal direction of maximum variance in the facial dataset (accounting for lighting, jaw shape, eye sockets, forehead width). Any face can be compactly expressed as a linear combination of the mean face plus weighted eigenfaces:

$$\Phi = \Gamma - \Psi = \sum_{k=1}^K \omega_k u_k$$

Mathematical Derivation: Turk & Pentland (1991) Technical Paper
Step 1: Face Vector Representation

Let each face image $I(x,y)$ be a 2D array of size $N \times N$. We flatten each image into a 1D column vector $\Gamma_i \in \mathbb{R}^D$ where $D = N^2$ (e.g., $64 \times 64 = 4096$). The training set contains $M$ images:

$$\{\Gamma_1, \Gamma_2, \dots, \Gamma_M\}$$
Step 2: Mean Face Vector & Difference Matrix

Compute the average face vector $\Psi$ across all $M$ training images, and subtract it from each face to mean-center the distribution:

$$\Psi = \frac{1}{M}\sum_{i=1}^M \Gamma_i, \qquad \Phi_i = \Gamma_i - \Psi$$ $$A = [\Phi_1, \Phi_2, \dots, \Phi_M] \in \mathbb{R}^{D \times M}$$
Step 3: The Turk-Pentland Covariance Reduction Trick (Key Mathematical Contribution)

The sample covariance matrix is $C = \frac{1}{M} \sum_{i=1}^M \Phi_i \Phi_i^T = \frac{1}{M} A A^T$. Notice that $C$ has dimensions $D \times D = 4096 \times 4096 = 16,777,216$ elements! Computing eigenvectors of $C$ directly is intractable. Turk & Pentland's Insight: Compute the eigenvectors of the $M \times M$ matrix $L = A^T A$ instead ($245 \times 245 = 60,025$ elements):

$$L v_k = \mu_k v_k \iff (A^T A) v_k = \mu_k v_k$$

Premultiplying both sides by $A$:

$$A (A^T A v_k) = \mu_k (A v_k) \iff (A A^T) (A v_k) = \mu_k (A v_k)$$

Therefore, $u_k = A v_k$ are the exact eigenvectors of the full covariance matrix $C = A A^T$! Normalizing to unit length:

$$u_k = \frac{A v_k}{\|A v_k\|_2}, \qquad k = 1, \dots, K$$
Step 4: Projection into Face Space

Any face $\Gamma$ is projected into the $K$-dimensional face subspace by subtracting the mean face and taking the inner product with each eigenface:

$$\omega_k = u_k^T (\Gamma - \Psi), \qquad \Omega = [\omega_1, \omega_2, \dots, \omega_K]^T = U^T (\Gamma - \Psi) \in \mathbb{R}^K$$
Step 5: The 4-Way Decision Rule (Section 3.2)

Given an input image $\Gamma$, we reconstruct the face $\Gamma_f = \Psi + U \Omega$ and compute two critical Euclidean distances:

$$\epsilon_d = \|\Gamma - \Gamma_f\|_2 \quad (\text{Distance from Face Space / DFFS})$$ $$\epsilon_c = \min_k \|\Omega - \Omega_k\|_2 \quad (\text{Distance in Face Space to Known Classes / DIFS})$$
Condition Decision Physical Meaning
$\epsilon_d \ge \theta_f$ NOT A FACE Input does not match face subspace geometry (e.g. background, noise, object)
$\epsilon_d < \theta_f \text{ and } \epsilon_c < \theta_c$ KNOWN FACE Confirmed human face, identified as enrolled subject $c^* = \arg\min \epsilon_k$
$\epsilon_d < \theta_f \text{ and } \epsilon_c \ge \theta_c$ UNKNOWN FACE Confirmed human face, but belongs to an unseen individual (can be enrolled!)

Answer: The image dimension $D = N^2$ (e.g. 4096) is vastly larger than the number of training samples $M$ (e.g. 245). The covariance matrix $C = \frac{1}{M} A A^T$ is of size $4096 \times 4096$ (over 16.7 million elements), making direct eigendecomposition computationally prohibitive and memory-intensive. Furthermore, since there are only $M$ training images, the rank of $A A^T$ is at most $M - 1$. By computing $L = A^T A$ of size $M \times M$ ($245 \times 245 = 60,025$ elements), we reduce computation by hundreds of times. Because $(A A^T)(A v_k) = \mu_k (A v_k)$, multiplying the eigenvectors of $L$ by $A$ gives the exact non-zero eigenvectors of $C$.
